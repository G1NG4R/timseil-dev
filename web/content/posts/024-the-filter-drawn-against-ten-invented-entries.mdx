The sheet for my log index is a good drawing. It has a mono list instead of
cards, a year separator with a count, a search field, and a row of tag chips:
`ALL 10`, `GO 04`, `DOCKER 02`, `KUBERNETES 01`, `POSTGRES 02`, `CI/CD 01`,
`LINUX 02`, `AWS 01`.

Eight chips. Under them, ten entries with titles like "Go concurrency patterns
in production" and "Reading a Postgres query plan without panicking".

The sheet says what those are, in its own design notes:

> Titel, Daten, Lesezeiten und Tag-Zähler sind Platzhalter.

I read that note eighteen days ago and filed it under "obviously". Then I built
the page.

## The corpus does not look like the drawing

```
entries          23
distinct tags    32
used once        17
```

Not eight chips. Thirty-two. More than half of them narrow a list of
twenty-three entries down to exactly one.

That is not a defect in the sheet. The sheet was drawn before I had written a
single entry, and it drew a plausible blog. It is a defect in the *reading* — the
moment where a placeholder count quietly becomes a design constraint because it
is the only number in front of you.

## The threshold that would have worked

The obvious repair is a threshold: only draw a chip for a tag that holds enough
entries to be worth filtering by. So I counted what each threshold would give.

```
>= 2 posts    15 chips
>= 3 posts     7 chips
>= 4 posts     5 chips
```

Seven. Plus the `ALL` sentinel, that is **eight chips** — exactly what the
artboard draws. testing 09, frontend 09, observability 06, css 06,
measurement 04, invariants 04, design-handoff 03.

For about a minute this felt like a discovery. The sheet's eight were not
arbitrary after all; there was a rule underneath, and I had found it.

There was not. The corpus has 23 entries today. At 30 entries the threshold gives
nine chips, at 60 it gives fifteen, and the row that "reproduces the sheet" stops
reproducing it the week after I write this. What the threshold actually does is
turn a drawing into a constant, and then let the constant drift away from the
drawing on its own.

And the number three measures nothing. It is not a width, not a count of
anything, not a limit anybody hit. It is the number that made the output match a
picture.

## What the neighbouring page already decided

I did not have to think this through from scratch, because the same question was
answered on this site three phases ago. `/work` has a chip row too, and its
vocabulary comes out of the API answer rather than out of a list:

> A chip that matches nothing is never drawn in the first place — the emptiness
> is a property of a COMBINATION, and it is the empty panel that carries it.

Every one of my thirty-two tags matches at least one entry. So every one of them
is a live control, and there is nothing to cap. The chip row wraps at a desk and
scrolls on a phone, and it is thirty-two chips wide because I have written about
thirty-two things.

That decision has a side effect I like: the empty panel on this page can now only
be reached one way. Since every chip carries a count derived from the corpus, a
tag on its own can never return nothing. Nought results is always the search box,
or a tag and a search together. The panel's wording says so, against a sheet that
says "Zu diesem Tag gibt es noch nichts" — a sentence that would contradict the
`06` printed on the chip beside it.

## The other thing the sheet contradicted

While I was in there: the search field's placeholder reads `grep titles…`, and
the script under it matches `r.textContent` — the whole row, including the ISO
date and the reading time.

Both cannot be right, and neither is what I wanted. Searching only titles loses
the reader who remembers half a sentence from a dek. Searching the whole row
makes `2026` return every entry I have ever written.

It searches the title, the dek and the tags: the fields the row actually draws.
The placeholder now says that.

## What I would do differently

I would have counted first. Not because the count changed the design much — a
mono list is still a mono list — but because the count is the thing that tells
you which parts of a drawing are decisions and which parts are furniture.

Eight chips looked like a decision for eighteen days. It was furniture. The
thirty-two are a measurement, and a measurement is the only kind of number this
site is allowed to print.
